# Public Evidence Map v0

StepHarbor is one instantiation of the broader uncertainty-aware control-plane thesis; this folder does not modify StepHarbor runtime behavior.

Reliable AI requires an uncertainty-aware control plane. The control plane converts uncertainty signals into explicit operational decisions: PROCEED, DEFER, ESCALATE, or BLOCK.

U1.1 and U1.2 catalogue public evidence sources only; they do not prove the thesis, implement a simulator, or validate a production system.

Many AI mistakes occur in situations where uncertainty should be explicitly managed, but this evidence map does not claim uncertainty explains every AI mistake.

## Source Notes

- U1-D001 RAGTruth: RAGTruth focuses on hallucination in retrieval-augmented generation. It contains nearly 18,000 naturally generated RAG responses from multiple LLMs, manually annotated at both case and word level with hallucination intensity. It spans summarization, question answering, and data-to-text generation using news, open-domain web QA, and structured Yelp-style business data sources.
- U1-D002 SQuAD 2.0: SQuAD 2.0 combines answerable SQuAD questions with more than 50,000 adversarially written unanswerable questions. Systems must determine when no answer is supported by the paragraph and abstain.
- U1-D003 AmbigQA / AmbigNQ: AmbigQA covers 14,042 NQ-open questions and requires finding every plausible answer plus rewriting the question to resolve ambiguity. The authors report that over half of NQ-open questions are ambiguous.
- U1-D004 FELM: FELM annotates generated text segments, includes predefined error types, and provides reference links that support or contradict claims across world knowledge, math, and reasoning.
- U1-D005 FActScore: FActScore decomposes generated long-form answers into atomic facts and measures what percentage are supported by reliable sources. The report reports that ChatGPT reached 58% factual precision on generated biographies in their human evaluation.
- U1-D006 SelfCheckGPT / WikiBio factuality annotations: SelfCheckGPT uses sampling consistency to detect hallucination: when a model knows a fact, sampled passages should be consistent; hallucinated facts tend to diverge or contradict each other. The authors manually annotated factuality of GPT-3-generated WikiBio passages.
- U1-D007 TruthfulQA: TruthfulQA includes 817 questions across 38 categories such as health, law, finance, and politics. The original study found the best model truthful on 58% of questions while humans reached 94%.
- U1-D008 CheXpert: CheXpert contains 224,316 chest radiographs from 65,240 patients, labels 14 observations from radiology reports, and captures uncertainty inherent in radiograph interpretation.
- U1-D009 WILDS: WILDS contains 10 datasets reflecting real-world distribution shifts, including shifts across hospitals, camera traps, satellite imaging, poverty mapping, molecular data, civil comments, and other domains. Standard training yields substantially worse out-of-distribution than in-distribution performance.
- U1-D010 Shifts Dataset: Shifts is built to evaluate uncertainty estimates and robustness under real distributional shift across weather prediction, machine translation, and self-driving vehicle motion prediction.
- U1-D011 AgentProcessBench: AgentProcessBench contains 1,000 diverse tool-using agent trajectories and 8,509 human-labeled step annotations, with reported 89.1% inter-annotator agreement.
- U1-D012 AgentErrorBench / AgentDebug: AgentDebug introduces AgentErrorTaxonomy across memory, reflection, planning, action, and system-level operations, and constructs annotated failure trajectories from ALFWorld, GAIA, and WebShop.
- U1-D013 AgentRx: AgentRx manually annotates failed agent runs with a critical failure step and category across structured API workflows, incident management, and open-ended web/file tasks.
- U1-D014 SpecTool / ToolScan: SpecTool characterizes seven LLM tool-use error patterns, addressing the gap that tool-use benchmarks often report only success rates without explaining failures.
- U1-D015 Pervasive Label Errors in Test Sets: This study estimates at least 3.3% label errors on average across 10 common datasets and at least 6% in ImageNet validation, with human validation of algorithmically flagged errors.

## Boundary

This v0 map is an evidence inventory only. It includes no simulator, no benchmark runner, no model calls, no dataset download, no dataset preprocessing, no calibration, no statistical assurance method, no conformal or CRC assurance method, no runtime routing, and no StepHarbor authorization change.
